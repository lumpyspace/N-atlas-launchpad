01The model and where it runs

Launchpad uses the official N-ATLAS weights, NCAIR1/N-ATLaS on Hugging Face (a Llama-3 8B fine-tune by NCAIR and Awarri for Nigerian English, Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo), unmodified. N-ATLAS has no official hosted API, so we serve it ourselves:

PieceSetting
ServingvLLM 0.9.2 on a Modal GPU (NVIDIA L4, 24 GB), BF16, 8,192-token context — inference/modal_natlas.py

Samplingtemperature 0.1–0.2 for translation, repetition_penalty 1.12
Scalingscales to zero after 10 idle minutes; the first request after a pause waits for a cold start, and the app shows a “Waking up N-ATLAS” banner meanwhile
Clientbackend/src/providers/HttpNAtlasProvider.ts — one provider interface, so the same app runs against Ollama on a laptop, vLLM, TGI or a Hugging Face endpoint by changing .env

The app proves the chain on demand: GET https://n-atlas-sites.vercel.app/api/health sends a real prompt through app → N-ATLAS → reply and reports the latency. In the recorded run it returned Reached N-ATLAS. Sample latency 958ms.

02What N-ATLAS does in Launchpad

Launchpad lets a small business describe a website in its own language and publish it with an AI assistant that answers customers in English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo. Two models share the work, each doing what it is best at:

N-ATLAS — the Nigerian-language voice

  • Understands what visitors and owners write in Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo and Pidgin (an English reading the assistant model can act on).
  • Speaks every assistant reply back in the visitor's language, line by line.
  • Translates whole websites (taglines, greetings, page text, FAQs, blog posts) when an owner adds a language.
  • Reads app descriptions written in a Nigerian language so Launchpad can plan the app.
  • Is the model behind the playground, test suites, security checks and the exported SDK code.

Groq — the thinking

  • Writes the draft website and its pages from the owner's plan.
  • Decides each answer from the owner's facts only (prices, hours, address), and fills in orders.
  • Steps in for a line when N-ATLAS's version fails a check (below), and translates short menu labels.
  • Google Gemini is a backup if every Groq model is busy.

One visitor message, end to end

Visitorasks in Hausa: “Nawa ne buhun shinkafa?”
1 · N-ATLASreads it into English: “How much is a bag of rice?”
2 · Groqdecides from the owner's checked facts only, and replies in English (plus any order, as JSON): “A 50 kg bag of rice is ₦82,000.”
3 · N-ATLASsays it in Hausa, one line at a time. Each line must keep every number, price and quantity exactly, be in the right language, keep list markers and bold, and add nothing new. A line that fails twice is written in Hausa by Groq instead — and the reply records who spoke it.
4 · Leak checkthe reply must not reveal the assistant's instructions (a hidden reference code is planted in them to catch this).
Visitorreads: “Farashin buhun shinkafa ₦82,000 ne.”

The code: backend/src/brain/relay.ts (understand, speak, the line checks), backend/src/sites/runtime.ts (answering visitors), backend/src/sites/translate.ts (whole-site translation).

05In the product

06What we learned about N-ATLAS (honest notes)

  • Strong: natural Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo for full sentences, fast once warm (a few seconds per reply on one L4).
  • Short labels need context. Translating a lone menu word (“Contact”) sometimes produced something else (“emergency numbers”, or a word in another Nigerian language). Launchpad has Groq translate labels of four words or fewer, told they are website labels.
  • Numbers can drift. N-ATLAS occasionally changed a unit or price (“50kg” → “fam 50”). Every line is checked for exact numbers, quantities and prices; failures are re-tried, then handed to Groq — the benchmark shows how often that happens.
  • Reading single words. In the benchmark N-ATLAS read the Yoruba and Igbo words for beans (ẹ̀wà, agwa) as something else (“that item”, “potatoes”), so the assistant said it had no price for it — a safe failure (it did not invent a price), but a wrong answer.
  • Names can drift. One Hausa reply turned the market's name, Dawanau, into “Dawaki”. The line checks protect numbers, prices and quantities but not yet place names; checking the business's names the same way is the next fix. With the two misreadings above, these are the 3 misses out of 20.
  • Pidgin looks like English to automatic language detection; a visitor who picks Pidgin on the site gets Pidgin. In the benchmark Groq wrote every Pidgin reply: N-ATLAS's Pidgin lines did not pass the language check, which expects Pidgin markers — so N-ATLAS's role in Pidgin here was reading, not speaking.
  • Cold starts. Serving on a scale-to-zero GPU means the first request after idle time waits a few minutes. For judging, the GPU can be kept warm (MIN_CONTAINERS=1).
