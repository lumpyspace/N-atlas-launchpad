03Live benchmark: real customer questions

We set up a sample shop with real details: a grain seller at Dawanau market, Kano, with rice at ₦82,000 per 50 kg bag and beans at ₦95,000 per 100 kg bag, maize with no price set, open 8am to 6pm Monday to Saturday. The script (scripts/bench.mjs) asks the questions a customer would actually send, in Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo, paced like real visitors. An answer is correct when the reply the customer reads carries the right fact, or, for maize, does not invent a price.

The misses

  • Hausa — “Nawa ne buhun shinkafa?”: the visitor read “Ina neman afuwar, ban da wannan bayanin ba. Don Allah ku tuntubi mai shi a WhatsApp akan +2348012345678 tsakanin karfe 8 na safe zuwa 6 na yamma don tambaya.” (English behind it: I am sorry, I do not have that information. Please contact the owner on WhatsApp at +2348012345678 between 8am and 6pm to ask.)
  • Hausa — “Nawa ne buhun wake?”: the visitor read “Ba ni da matakan hannun jari na yanzu don wake. Don Allah ku tuntubi mai shi ta WhatsApp a +2348012345678 tsakanin karfe 8 na safe zuwa 6 na yamma domin duba samuwarsa.” (English behind it: I don't have the current stock levels for beans. Please contact the owner on WhatsApp at +2348012345678 between 8am and 6pm to check availability.)
  • Yoruba — “Elo ni àpò ẹ̀wà?”: the visitor read “Ma binu, ṣugbọn emi ko ta awon ohun mimu. Mo n ta iresi, ewa, ati oka nikan. O le ṣayẹwo awọn idiyele fun awọn nkan wọnyẹn ti o ba fẹ.” (English behind it: I am sorry, but I do not sell sweets. I only sell rice, beans, and maize. You can check the prices for those items if you like.)
  • Igbo — “Ego ole ka akpa agwa bụ?”: the visitor read “Anaghị m ere poteto. Anọ m na-ere osikapa, agwa, na akpu. Ị ga-achọ ọnụ ahịa maka otu n'ime ha?” (English behind it: I do not sell potatoes. I sell rice, beans, and maize. Would you like the price for one of those?)

No answer invented a price. Every miss was a misreading where the assistant then said it did not have the information, which is the safe way to be wrong.

04What testing changed

We ran the live tests repeatedly on 12 October 2026, fixing what each run exposed. These are the findings that changed the product:

What we foundEvidenceWhat we changed
N-ATLAS could not produce usable Nigerian Pidgin.In the first benchmark (20 questions, including 5 in Pidgin) N-ATLAS wrote none of the 5 Pidgin replies; every one had to be rewritten by Groq, and a Pidgin question was once answered in English.Dropped Pidgin as an offered language. Launchpad now offers N-ATLAS's four official languages; Pidgin messages are still understood and answered in English.
Smaller AI models write unreadable Yoruba and Igbo.When Groq's main model was busy, a smaller one replied in Yoruba with text that made no sense, and in Igbo called rice “ụka” (church).Only Groq's strongest model may read or write Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo. The smaller models help with English work only.
A busy model led to invented answers.With every model at its limit, replies fell back to N-ATLAS answering alone, which said the shop opens “Monday to Friday” (it opens to Saturday), invented a 20 kg bag, and made up the rest of the conversation.A line no model can help with now stays in English with its facts intact, and N-ATLAS's own answers are cut where they start inventing. Covered by a new automated test.
The free Groq plan runs out under load.Groq's free plan allows about 8,000 tokens a minute per model; the build guide and the website draft competed for it, and sites fell back to a basic template.English work goes to smaller models first; Simple mode writes only the two plan sections owners read. Sites are drafted by Groq again (screenshot in the integration evidence).
N-ATLAS misreads some single words.“Beans” in Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo; Hausa “nawa ne” (how much) read as “how many”.We tested giving N-ATLAS the shop's item list while reading. It fixed some cases and broke others, so we did not ship it. Recorded as a known limit.
Publishing hung on the live site.Saving a site waited 5 minutes and then failed: a setting had been pasted with quotation marks, and the error was never reported.Settings are cleaned automatically, database calls time out after 10 seconds, and failures show a clear message.

05Automated tests

181 tests run in seconds without a GPU or API keys, using fake models that behave like the real ones, including fakes that fail on purpose. Several were added because of the findings above: the safe English fallback when no model is free, keeping smaller models away from Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo, naming projects described in Pidgin, and publishing when the database fails.
